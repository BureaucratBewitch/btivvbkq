1. How do I do a mason jar soil test at home?

Fill a clear quart jar about one-third with soil from 4–6 inches down (not surface mulch). Add water nearly to the top, optional dish soap, shake hard 2–3 minutes, then leave undisturbed. Sand settles first, then silt, then clay overnight. Mark each band; each height ÷ total soil height = its percentage. Aim for loam near 40% sand / 40% silt / 20% clay.

2. My jar shows mostly clay — what should I add this weekend?

If clay is roughly 35%+, favor coarse compost or aged manure, avoid tilling when wet, and consider raised beds. Sand alone rarely fixes clay. Amend one maintainable bed (~32–96 sq ft) instead of the whole yard.

3. Are backyard chickens worth it on a suburban lot?

Often only if you value freshness and manure loops — not pure grocery egg math. For a 4-hen flock, feed commonly runs about $150–$250/year after modest scrap credits; egg grocery credit may be similar unless local prices are high. Coop amortization and legality decide year one.

4. Can I keep chickens if I have an HOA?

Maybe. City code and HOA covenants are separate. Check ordinances for flock caps, setbacks, and rooster bans; pull CC&Rs for livestock / poultry / coop / nuisance language; get written architectural approval when required. Decide on paper before buying birds.

5. How do I set up one rain barrel on a suburban lot?

One food-grade barrel on a clean downspout: screened inlet, reachable spigot, overflow away from the foundation. Rough math: ~600 gal per inch of rain on 1,000 sq ft of roof — a 55-gal barrel is a buffer. Check local/HOA rules; drain or disconnect before hard freeze.

7. What should I try in the first two weekends?

Weekend 1: mason jar soil test + one maintainable bed layout (~32–96 sq ft). Weekend 2: either one rain barrel done right or chickens research only (rules + math — no impulse flock). Enough signal to use a 60-day guarantee without theater.

8. What should I plant and prep in fall 2026?

Garlic, a last cool-season greens sowing, a soil check before frost, one rain barrel before peak wet weeks, and greenhouse / cold-frame cover. Keep it weekend-sized.

9. How much land do I need for backyard self-reliance?

A common Melchiore-style planning number is about 1,020 sq ft per person with intensive beds — far less than a 40-acre fantasy. Start with soil diagnosis and one bed you can maintain, then add water and protein projects that fit real weekends.

10. What is an earth-tube greenhouse loop?

Buried pipes that move greenhouse air through steadier soil temperatures (often near ~55°F at depth). Useful after orientation, seals, and thermal mass are solid — it will not rescue a leaky or poorly sited structure.
